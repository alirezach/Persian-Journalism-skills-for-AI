# Spelling, homophones and names

Work from meaning, morphology and established usage, not just visual familiarity. The compilation's phonetic and alphabetic explanations help identify why a spelling is plausible; they do not make every unfamiliar word a typo.

## Ordinary words

Check the intended word in its sentence before choosing among homophones. Distinguish an inflection from part of the lexical root. For two accepted spellings, prefer consistency with the user's style; do not mark the other form inherently wrong. A word with several meanings may need different equivalents in different contexts. Do not use a thesaurus to vary a technical term merely to avoid repetition.

## Names and foreign material

Preserve personal, institutional, geographic, historical and bibliographic names when their verified form is unavailable. A modern standard form need not replace a historically meaningful spelling. If the user provides an authoritative spelling, apply it consistently to eligible mentions while leaving exact quotations and source strings intact. Add an original-language form only when supplied or separately verified, not from a guessed transliteration.

Arabic passages and Arabic names must not undergo general Persian letter normalization when their exact spelling is part of the evidence. Established Persian loanword spellings can follow the selected house style outside protected spans. Keep uncertainty about an OCR-damaged name visible; do not silently replace it with a more familiar person.

## Decision record

For a consequential correction record the original form, chosen form, basis and exceptions when a log is requested. For long works, maintain a name/term ledger and check captions, footnotes and references as well as body text. Consistency is subordinate to identity: two similar-looking names must not be merged without evidence.

Synthetic example: if the brief explicitly establishes «پژوهشکدهٔ سپهر» as the institution's name, an ordinary-text typo can be corrected to that supplied form. The same correction is not automatically authorized inside a facsimile caption quoting a historical document. For an ambiguous homophone, leave the word and ask which meaning is intended rather than delivering a confident but different proposition.
